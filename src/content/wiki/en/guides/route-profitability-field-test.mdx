## What does route profitability mean?

Route profitability asks how much useful base improvement a route produces for the time and risk it consumes. The rarest egg, shortest outward sprint, and largest pet card are incomplete measures on their own. A usable comparison includes the entire loop, failed attempts, and the value of the pet slot that actually changes.

This guide does not provide a hidden spawn rate or a universal winning route. It shows how to create evidence from your own current session. The result applies to the routes, conditions, and time window you record.

## What should you prepare before testing?

Choose two routes that are realistic at your current speed. Identify the start and end as the same repeatable state: standing at the base and ready to leave. Decide what counts as success before starting. Usually, a success means the target returned and the loop finished, not merely reaching a nest or picking up an egg.

Prepare a simple sheet with these columns:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Route | A stable name or destination |
| Attempt | Sequential number, including failures |
| Start and end time | Full loop, ready-to-leave to ready-to-leave |
| Outcome | Returned, lost, missed, or aborted |
| Egg or pet | Visible identity if obtained |
| Slot before and after | Displayed contribution under matched conditions |
| Conditions | Guardian cue, traffic, visible event, interruption |

Do not record usernames, chat, account identifiers, or other personal information. The test needs route conditions, not player identity.

## How do you run a fair route sample?

Scout each route without a valuable egg. Observe at least one guardian cycle, select a visible launch cue, and practice the return path. Then alternate routes or test them in comparable blocks so one is not measured only during a quiet period and the other only during congestion.

Start the timer when leaving the base. Stop when the attempt is complete and you are ready to leave again. Record failures with their full elapsed time and zero returned value. If you stop to change settings or answer a message, mark the interruption rather than silently adding it to route performance.

Run enough attempts to see ordinary variation. Five attempts can expose a route problem but cannot establish a permanent game probability. Ten or more clean attempts make a personal planning comparison more stable. Keep the sample-size claim modest and tied to what you actually recorded.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not keep only the wins">Removing failed or slow attempts turns the result into a highlight reel. Keep every eligible attempt or define the exclusion rule before testing.</Callout>

## How do you calculate successful returns per hour?

Add the elapsed time for every eligible attempt, including failures. Divide successful returns by total hours:

`successful returns per hour = successful returns ÷ total observed hours`

If you observed minutes, divide total minutes by 60 first. This result describes your sample. It is not the egg's official spawn chance and should not be presented as one.

Use a range when the route varies widely. You can compare the first half and second half of the sample or calculate separate quiet and crowded blocks. If the conclusion changes between them, server conditions are a meaningful part of the decision.

## How do you value the returned pet?

Compare the pet with the slot it would replace. The immediate improvement is:

`slot improvement = candidate final contribution − replaced slot contribution`

Use values visible under matching conditions. If a card already includes mutation or size, do not multiply it again. If the candidate does not improve any slot, record zero immediate slot improvement even though it may have collection or unlock value.

For mixed results, calculate each distinct returned configuration separately. The [income calculator](/tools/income-calculator) can reproduce the arithmetic, while the [All Pets Index](/pets/all-pets) helps identify tracked records and evidence limits.

## How do you combine time and improvement?

For the observed window, add the slot improvements that would actually be used and divide by total test hours. Call this “observed useful improvement per hour,” not passive income per hour or a guaranteed future rate. The measure is specific to your current base because another player may replace a different slot.

A route can also have non-income value, such as learning a biome or pursuing an unlock. Record that in a separate column rather than converting it into invented currency. Keeping goals separate makes the conclusion readable.

## How do you compare Route A with Route B?

Compare sample size, total observed time, successful returns, failure reasons, and useful slot improvement. Then ask whether the difference survives normal variation. If Route A wins only because of one exceptional result, run another block before changing the plan. If Route B stays useful across both quiet and crowded conditions, its reliability may matter more than the best single return.

Do not compare a fastest run from one route with an all-attempt average from the other. Use the same calculation and inclusion rule for both.

## What should the conclusion look like?

A responsible conclusion states the scope and the next action. For example: “In this dated sample, Route A produced more successful returns under the recorded conditions, while Route B had fewer failures. I will repeat Route A after the next speed checkpoint.” This wording reports an observation without inventing universal superiority.

Include the date, current speed, sample count, and any event that could affect the route. If you publish the result, link the raw de-identified table or summarize every attempt rather than showing only the outcome.

## When should you repeat the test?

Repeat after a speed change, route or guardian update, new biome, altered carrying behavior, meaningful base improvement, or persistent disagreement with the earlier result. Preserve the old sample with its date so the change can be explained.

Use the [speed training guide](/guides/speed-training) when movement is the bottleneck and the [safe stealing guide](/guides/how-to-steal-eggs) when failures come from timing or route choice. Report public data conflicts through [Contact](/contact); proposed observations are reviewed before becoming wiki claims.

## How do you control common sources of bias?

Route tests are easy to distort without noticing. Alternating routes helps prevent one route from receiving all of the early practice attempts and the other receiving all of the later, more skilled attempts. Using the same target class keeps an unusually valuable or easy egg from deciding the comparison by itself. Recording interrupted runs prevents a test from quietly discarding inconvenient failures. If a server event, obvious lag spike, or player blockade changes only one attempt, label it before deciding whether to include a second view with that attempt excluded.

Keep practice separate from measurement. Walk both routes empty until the turns are familiar, then begin the recorded sample. Do not change gear, speed, target rules, or stopping conditions halfway through unless you end the first sample and label a new one. A fair test does not require perfect laboratory control; it requires enough notes to explain what differed.

## How should uncertainty appear in the final comparison?

Report the numerator and denominator instead of only a percentage. “Six successful returns in ten attempts” shows more than “60% success,” especially beside a different sample size. Include median or typical trip time as well as the fastest run so one lucky path does not dominate the hourly estimate. If the value of a returned pet is unknown, publish successful returns per hour separately and leave the value calculation open.

The conclusion should name the tested conditions and its practical limit. For example, one route may be the better training choice under your current speed while the other could become preferable after failures fall. That is a useful finding even when it does not produce a permanent global winner.
