## How do you measure speed gain?

1. Record your current speed.
2. Train continuously for at least 30 seconds.
3. Record the new speed.
4. Divide the increase by the sample duration.
5. Repeat once and use the lower or median rate.

## What is the time formula?

`time remaining = (target speed − current speed) ÷ measured gain per second`

Enter the values in the [Speed Goal Calculator](/tools/calculators#speed-calculator).

## What should count as one training sample?

A sample begins when continuous treadmill training starts and ends when it stops. Record the starting speed, ending speed, and elapsed seconds from the same session. Do not mix an old starting value with a new timer, and pause the sample if you leave the treadmill. A longer clean sample reduces the influence of reaction time when starting and stopping the clock.

Repeat the sample under similar visible conditions. If the results are close, use the median. If they are far apart, inspect the conditions before averaging them. A temporary boost, a different treadmill, an interruption, or a copied digit can explain the difference. Keep the unusual result in your notes, but do not let one unexplained high result define the schedule.

## How do you choose between training and earning?

Training has an opportunity cost: time on the treadmill is time you are not completing egg returns. The right choice depends on the current bottleneck. If your base still has empty slots or obvious low-income replacements, another reliable egg may improve progress immediately. If every useful next route is blocked by movement and existing routes no longer offer meaningful upgrades, training becomes easier to justify.

Compare two plans over the same amount of real time. Plan A uses the current route and records expected successful returns. Plan B spends part of that time training, then tests the next route. Include a failure allowance in both. The comparison is personal because server traffic, route knowledge, and current pets differ between players.

## How large should the safety margin be?

There is no universal percentage that works for every route. Build the margin from observed problems. Start at the visible gate, make a low-value test, and note where time is lost: a turn, guardian pressure, carrying movement, or other players. Increase the target until the route has enough spare time for the errors you actually encounter.

Keep entry speed and comfortable route speed as separate notes. Publishing one number without its purpose creates the conflict seen in many public tables. When sharing a result, say whether it means “gate accepted this value,” “completed one return,” or “completed repeated returns with a buffer.”

## What is a useful daily training routine?

Use short checkpoints rather than one distant promise:

1. Measure one baseline sample at the start.
2. Train for the productive minutes you planned.
3. Record the end value without rounding away a meaningful difference.
4. Recalculate the remaining time with the new current speed.
5. Stop when the next planned route test becomes available.
6. Test with a replaceable egg before risking a valuable target.

If a session underperforms, record why and continue from the actual value. Do not “correct” the log to match yesterday's forecast. The forecast exists to help schedule effort; it is not a score you need to beat.

## How should mobile or interrupted play be handled?

Phone notifications, frame drops, touch controls, and short play windows can reduce productive time even when the underlying gain formula is unchanged. Measure on the device and connection you normally use. If you switch platforms, collect a new sample instead of assuming the desktop rate and route handling transfer perfectly.

For fragmented sessions, add only the time actually spent training. Five six-minute samples can support a daily total, but five sessions that merely stayed open for six minutes cannot. A simple note with start, stop, gain, and interruption reason is enough.

## When should you remeasure?

Remeasure after an update that mentions movement, progression, treadmills, passes, or boosts; after a visible upgrade changes; or when repeated results stop matching the plan. Also remeasure before publishing a recommendation to another player. The article's date proves when the text was edited, while your sample date proves when the rate was observed.

The [dedicated speed calculator](/tools/speed-calculator) can turn this sample into a daily schedule. For the next step, time an actual return route with [How to Steal Eggs Safely](/guides/how-to-steal-eggs) so speed training stays connected to a playable goal.

Keep the raw measurements so a later estimate can be reproduced instead of relying on memory.

## How do you decide whether a training plan is working?

Compare planned productive minutes with what you actually complete, then remeasure the displayed gain after several ordinary sessions. If the rate is close but the schedule slips, change the daily time assumption. If the rate itself changes, look for a visible upgrade, temporary boost, different treadmill, interruption, or patch before replacing the original sample. Do not average unlike conditions without labeling them.

A useful plan has an exit rule. Stop or revise when the target gate changes, the route becomes reliably safe, another upgrade gives a better return for the same time, or repeated samples no longer support the chosen rate. The goal is enough verified movement capacity for the next task, not an impressive number detached from play.
