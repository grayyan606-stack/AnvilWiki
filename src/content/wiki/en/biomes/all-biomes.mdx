## Why are exact speed numbers not hardcoded here?

Recent sources disagree. For example, Cherry Blossom access is reported around 2.5B to 3.5B speed depending on the guide and whether the number means entry, outrunning the guardian, or a safer carrying target.

## What does progression order tell you?

The order identifies the sequence of areas described by current sources. It does not supply a permanent speed table, guarantee that every player sees identical conditions, or say that entering an area means its eggs can be returned safely. Use it to locate the next area to inspect in the live game.

Cherry Blossom appears after the earlier nine-area sequence, which explains why older guides stop at Cosmic. When comparing sources, first ask whether they include the update before treating their total as a conflict.

## How should you verify a gate?

Approach the next biome in the official experience and record the visible requirement or response. Note current speed, date, and whether the check shows access only. If the gate accepts entry, make a low-value route test and separately record pickup and return outcomes.

A screenshot of a gate should preserve its label and unit. A cropped number without the biome name cannot safely update the table. If no value is visible, describe the test as an observation rather than inventing an exact requirement.

## Why can two speed recommendations both be honest?

One source may report the minimum accepted at the boundary. Another may recommend enough speed to outrun pressure while carrying an egg. A third may include a personal margin for crowded servers. Those are different questions.

Label numbers by purpose:

| Label | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Entry check | Accessed the area under recorded conditions |
| Return test | Completed a particular route with an egg |
| Safety target | Personal planning margin over a known value |

Never average these into a supposed official threshold. The [Biome Readiness Checker](/tools/calculators#biome-calculator) keeps the observed minimum and chosen margin separate.

## How do you plan the next biome efficiently?

Identify the weakest useful outcome available in the new area and compare it with the best repeatable route you already have. Estimate training time from a current treadmill sample. Then test whether the potential improvement justifies leaving a route that already succeeds.

This avoids training toward the final biome only because it is last in the list. The next useful zone is the one that removes your current bottleneck, not necessarily the newest headline area.

## What should a route test record?

Time from the base until you are ready to begin another attempt. Record empty and carrying runs separately, plus failures. Note the guardian cue, route used, visible boosts, server congestion, and egg size when relevant. Use the median normal successful time for planning, while keeping failed attempts in the overall evaluation.

The [safe stealing guide](/guides/how-to-steal-eggs) provides the complete route process. The [Egg Run Planner](/tools/calculators#run-calculator) can compare your loop with a measured cycle without claiming a universal spawn schedule.

## When should you remain in the current biome?

Stay when the next area is accessible but not repeatable, when existing pet slots still have easy improvements, or when training cost exceeds the likely near-term gain. A new biome does not erase the value of a reliable older route.

Move on when returns are stable, the current target pool no longer improves the base efficiently, and the next route has enough margin to test without risking scarce eggs. Record the reason so the decision can be revisited after an update.

## How are biome changes maintained here?

A new or reordered biome needs current identity evidence, an updated sequence, internal links, and a dated correction. Changed speed advice needs its definition as well as its number. Editors should update the relevant article evidence note and [data corrections log](/updates/data-corrections), not merely change a search description.

Readers can submit a de-identified gate or route observation through [Contact](/contact). The report is reviewed before publication, and missing values remain unknown until the claim can be supported.

## What should you write down during a biome review?

Use a short record that another player could repeat. Note the date, the official experience you joined, the biome you started in, the next visible destination, your displayed speed, whether you carried an egg, and where the attempt failed or succeeded. Describe the gate in the words shown by the game instead of translating it into an assumed formula. If the interface displays no number, record that absence rather than inventing a precise threshold from one successful run.

Separate observation from advice. “The gate displayed this requirement” is an observation. “Train beyond it before carrying a large egg” is a safety recommendation that depends on route length, device control, lag, and interruption risk. Keeping those statements separate lets an editor update the underlying fact without erasing useful strategy. It also helps readers decide whether an older route still applies after a patch.
