## How to use this biome hub

Steal an Egg progression is not just a list of places. Each biome changes the risk calculation: the route is longer, the guardian can be more punishing, the eggs can be larger, and other players can interfere at the worst moment. A number that gets you through the gate is not the same as a number that lets you carry a valuable egg home.

Use this page as the planning layer. Start with the order, identify the next realistic target, then use the calculators and route checklist before you commit training time. If you need the compact threshold table, open [All Biomes](/biomes/all-biomes). If you are preparing for the newest zone, use the [Cherry Blossom biome guide](/biomes/cherry-blossom).

## What is the current biome order?

| Order | Biome          | Planning role                                    |
| ----: | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
|     1 | Forest         | Learning route, first eggs, low-pressure warmup  |
|     2 | Lake           | First meaningful speed gate and route discipline |
|     3 | Desert         | First large jump in required planning            |
|     4 | Jungle         | Midgame area with more pressure                  |
|     5 | Snow           | Late-midgame targets and colder route risk       |
|     6 | Volcano        | Guardian pressure and return-route risk increase |
|     7 | Abyss Ocean    | Late-game speed requirement                      |
|     8 | Prehistoric    | Major progression jump                           |
|     9 | Cosmic         | Former endgame ceiling                           |
|    10 | Cherry Blossom | Current post-update endgame zone                 |

The order is useful for planning, but it should not be read as a guarantee that every source uses the same wording for "access." Some players use a biome gate to mean reaching the zone, others mean escaping with a normal egg, and others mean escaping with a large egg after a busy server.

## How should you read a biome speed number?

Treat every public speed threshold as a reference point, not a law. Sources may disagree because the game updates, because server timing changes, and because a route has more than one possible success condition. Cherry Blossom is a good example: current community reports place practical needs around **2.5B to 3.5B speed**, and that range should stay a range.

Before relying on a number, ask three questions:

1. Does the source distinguish entry speed from carrying speed?
2. Is the number tied to a date and game version?
3. Does it account for route mistakes, guardian pressure, and egg size?

If any answer is no, use the number as a floor and add a personal safety margin.

## How to plan a route in any biome

1. **Set the target.** Choose the next biome or a personal speed target above the reported gate.
2. **Time the full loop.** Measure base-to-target-to-base, not only the distance to the egg.
3. **Add the escape buffer.** Include guardian avoidance, traffic, carrying slowdown, and recovery after a mistake.
4. **Record several samples.** Use the median, not the best possible run.
5. **Test with a lower-value egg first.** Do not risk your best target before the route is stable.
6. **Recheck after an update.** New pets, biomes, events, or movement changes can invalidate an old route.

## Biome-by-biome planning notes

### Forest

Forest is the learning zone. The goal is not only to hatch an early pet, but also to learn spawn timing, base protection, and the habit of timing a full loop. Practice the same route repeatedly so later biome plans are built on a measured route time rather than a feeling.

### Lake

Lake is where the first meaningful speed gate appears. Use it to build the planning routine that matters later: record your current speed, time the route, and leave a buffer. A slow, repeatable route is usually better than a fast route that fails under pressure.

### Desert

Desert is a good checkpoint for whether your speed training is keeping up with progression. If you are only just reaching the gate, treat the area as practice. If you can complete several safe runs, you are ready to consider higher-value targets.

### Jungle

Jungle marks the transition into more demanding midgame routes. The extra pressure means a small planning error is more expensive. Keep egg value conservative until you can complete the route without relying on a lucky escape.

### Snow

Snow is often a good late-midgame target because stronger pets appear here, but the same rule applies. A high-value egg is only profitable if the route remains safe. Compare the value you expect from the run with the time you spend training and traveling.

### Volcano

Volcano raises both reward and risk. The return leg deserves more attention than the trip out. Before carrying a valuable egg, rehearse the escape path and make sure your speed target includes enough margin for guardian pressure.

### Abyss Ocean

Abyss Ocean is late-game progression. At this point, small route improvements can be worth more than chasing another small speed increase. Time the loop, identify where you lose speed, and practice a conservative escape line before using the area for valuable eggs.

### Prehistoric

Prehistoric is a major jump. Do not treat a stale threshold as an admission ticket. Use a lower-risk egg until you know your actual completion rate, then decide whether the biome is worth routine farming.

### Cosmic

Cosmic was the former endgame ceiling. It remains a benchmark for whether your speed, route discipline, and pet income are ready for the newest zone. If Cosmic routes feel unstable, prioritize consistency before moving on.

### Cherry Blossom

Cherry Blossom is the current endgame zone and introduces Cherry Blossom pets, including Crane, plus the Sakura Incubator and Great Bloom event systems. Because sources disagree on exact speed requirements, plan a personal safety target and test the route before committing rare resources. More details are in the [Cherry Blossom guide](/biomes/cherry-blossom).

## How to train speed without wasting time

The core formula is simple:

`time remaining = (target speed − current speed) ÷ measured gain per second`

The hard part is measuring gain honestly. Train continuously for at least 30 seconds, repeat the test, and use the lower or median result. Enter that value into the [Speed Goal Calculator](/tools/calculators#speed-calculator), then decide whether the next biome is worth the minutes per day you actually have.

If the estimate is long, do not abandon progression. Break it into daily blocks, use the [training guide](/guides/speed-training), and compare your expected income from the next biome with the time cost.

## How to avoid outdated biome data

A fast-updating Roblox experience can make an old table dangerous. Older guides may describe nine biomes, while newer post-update coverage includes Cherry Blossom as the tenth. Speed thresholds can also shift between reports. This wiki keeps known conflicts visible instead of converting them into a fake exact answer.

When you compare sources, prefer the live in-game gate, then a dated post-update source, then a transparent calculator. Read the [source policy](/wiki/source-policy) and the [data corrections log](/updates/data-corrections) before trusting a biome value.

## Biome planning FAQ

### What speed should I aim for?

Aim for the highest credible reported minimum for your next biome, then add enough margin for your route and egg size. If two sources disagree, use the higher number as a temporary planning target until you can test it.

### Why can I enter a biome but still fail?

Entry and escape are different checks. You may have enough speed to enter the area, but not enough to carry a larger egg through guardian pressure, traffic, and route mistakes.

### Should I train before or after improving pets?

Do both in small cycles. Pets improve passive income and route reward, while speed unlocks safer targets. If you cannot complete the next route safely, train speed first. If the route is comfortable but income is low, improve pets.

### How often should routes be rechecked?

Recheck after every major update and whenever your completion rate changes. Keep the last check date on your own notes so you do not trust a route that worked before a patch.
