## How do you unlock the Sakura Incubator?

1. Reach the Cherry Blossom biome.
2. Steal and hatch a **Crane**, an Epic pet associated with the zone.
3. Bring the Crane to the Incubator and submit it to unlock the system.
4. Wait for the Great Bloom event and collect Sakura Crystals.
5. Insert an unhatched egg, charge the machine, and run the mutation attempt.

## What outcomes are currently reported?

| Outcome | Multiplier | Reported chance |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Bloom | 1.5× | 97.5% |
| Spirit Bloom | 3× | 2.5% |

<Callout type="warn" title="Update-sensitive odds">These percentages come from recent community reporting, not a permanent guarantee. Check the live Incubator UI after patches.</Callout>

## How does the Great Bloom event work?

Current reporting describes a 30-minute event cycle and an active window of about three minutes. Players use that window to collect Sakura Crystals, so arriving before the event starts avoids losing time to travel.

Sources: [Beebom Sakura Incubator guide](https://beebom.com/steal-an-egg-sakura-incubator-guide/) and [GameBoost's current summary](https://gameboost.com/blog/steal-an-egg-sakura-incubator).

## What should you verify before sacrificing a Crane?

Inspect the live Incubator prompt and confirm that it names the Crane requirement and explains what submission does. Current guides agree on the unlock, but a resource-consuming action deserves a current interface check. Record the date, prompt, Crane state, and visible result before proceeding.

Do not rely on a screenshot that shows only a pet and machine without the requirement text. It may document someone else's completed unlock rather than the rule that applies to your account. If the prompt differs from this guide, stop and report the conflict.

## How do you plan a Great Bloom session?

Reach Cherry Blossom early enough to observe the live event signal. Learn a short crystal route before the active window and record the resource total before and after. Public timing reports are useful for preparation, but server state and updates can make a fixed countdown inaccurate.

Use three separate timestamps: when you began observing, when the event became active, and when it ended. A single arrival time cannot establish the full cycle because the server may already be between events. Repeated observations on clearly identified sessions provide a better planning range.

## What should you record about Sakura Crystals?

Record the visible starting and ending balance, active event state, route, and time spent collecting. If a cost appears in the Incubator UI, preserve the label and unit. Do not infer a universal collection rate from one crowded or unusually quiet server.

When comparing routes, include unsuccessful collection time. The fastest successful pass alone overstates what a normal session will produce.

## Which egg should receive an attempt?

Begin with the egg or expected pet's current base value and your reason for upgrading it. A multiplier creates a larger absolute change on a larger base, but the best choice also depends on whether the pet would replace a productive slot and whether the egg is eligible under the live rules.

Create two conditional calculations, one for each reported outcome. These show possible contributions, not a guarantee. Use the [Mutation Comparison](/tools/calculators#mutation-calculator) and avoid applying the multiplier again if the live card already includes it.

## How should the reported probabilities affect expectations?

The table reproduces current community reporting and labels it update-sensitive. A 2.5% report does not mean every fortieth personal attempt must succeed, and a short streak cannot verify or disprove the exact rate. Probability describes a long-run model, while your resource decision happens over a limited number of attempts.

Plan using an outcome you can tolerate. If receiving Bloom would make the egg a bad use of resources, do not value the attempt as though Spirit Bloom were certain. Check for a current percentage in the live interface before relying on the public figures.

## How do you confirm the result?

After the attempt, inspect the pet card or result screen for the mutation name and displayed contribution. Record the pre-attempt egg, visible cost, mutation, size, and final value. A visual effect without a label may be insufficient to distinguish similarly themed outcomes.

If the arithmetic differs from your expectation, check whether the displayed value already includes mutation and size. Do not rewrite the public multiplier from one unexplained card.

## What are the most common planning errors?

- Arriving after an event begins and treating partial observation as the full duration.
- Submitting a Crane without reading the current unlock prompt.
- Spending crystals on an egg with no clear upgrade purpose.
- Valuing every attempt at the rarest possible outcome.
- Multiplying an already modified card value a second time.
- Publishing a personal streak as an official probability.

Correct one uncertainty before committing the next resource. The Incubator is a sequence of gate, event, currency, input, chance, and result; evidence for one step does not prove all the others.
